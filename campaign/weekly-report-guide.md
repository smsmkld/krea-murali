# Weekly KPI report: guide and template

## How to use this file
When the owner says "we need a report for [client]", write the report from this template and guide.
1. Ask for any numbers not already given (emails sent, replies, positive replies, calls booked, this week and last week).
2. Fill in the template, then write "What we saw", "What we're doing about it" and "What we need from you".
3. Add a Loom script if asked (see "The Loom").
4. Output the report as a file or in chat. Never send it to anyone; the owner sends it.

## Cadence and format
Every Friday, every client, without exception. Format: a short Loom plus the numbers pasted into the Slack channel. About 15 minutes per client once the sheet is built.

The rule that makes this work: send it on the bad weeks too. A report that only shows up when numbers are good teaches the client that silence means bad news. Then every quiet week becomes anxiety, and anxious clients churn.

## The template
```
WEEKLY REPORT · [Client] · WEEK OF [date]
[Loom link]

                        THIS WEEK   LAST WEEK
New campaigns launched      [ ]         [ ]
Emails sent                 [ ]         [ ]
Total replies               [ ]         [ ]
Positive replies            [ ]         [ ]
Calls booked                [ ]         [ ]
Positive → call rate        [ ]%        [ ]%

WHAT WE SAW
[Your read on the data. What's working, what isn't, and what you
think is driving it.]

WHAT WE'RE DOING ABOUT IT
  → Scaling: [campaign name] · [why]
  → Cutting: [campaign name] · [why]
  → Testing next week: [new angle]

WHAT WE NEED FROM YOU
  □ [the specific thing] · by [date]
```

## Example: good week
```
WEEKLY REPORT · Northwind Logistics · WEEK OF March 10
https://loom.com/share/...

                        THIS WEEK   LAST WEEK
New campaigns launched      2           1
Emails sent                 4,850       4,200
Total replies               96          71
Positive replies            11          7
Calls booked                5           3
Positive → call rate        45%         43%

WHAT WE SAW
Best week so far. The jump came almost entirely from the "3PL ops
manager" angle we launched Monday · 7 of the 11 positive replies came
from that one campaign, off only 1,400 sends. That's a much higher rate
than anything else running.

Read on why: it's the first angle where we led with the Delta case study
instead of the general pitch. Same audience, different proof, roughly
triple the positive reply rate.

The "freight broker" campaign is now at 6,200 sends with 2 positive
replies. That's enough volume to call it. Killing it.

WHAT WE'RE DOING ABOUT IT
  → Scaling: 3PL ops manager angle, expanding the list from 1,400 to
    4,000 and adding two more script variants
  → Cutting: freight broker campaign, no signal at volume
  → Testing next week: same 3PL angle but targeting Directors of Ops
    at 200+ headcount, since the wins skew larger than we expected

WHAT WE NEED FROM YOU
  □ Feedback on the 3 calls from last week · by Monday. Two of them
    I'm not sure were the right fit and I want to tighten targeting
  □ Any newer case study in food and bev? The Delta one is working and
    I'd like a second proof point for the scaled version · by Wednesday
```

## Example: bad week
This is the one worth studying. Most people go quiet in this week, and going quiet is what kills the account.
```
WEEKLY REPORT · Meridian Consulting · WEEK OF March 10
https://loom.com/share/...

                        THIS WEEK   LAST WEEK
New campaigns launched      1           2
Emails sent                 4,100       4,400
Total replies               38          52
Positive replies            2           6
Calls booked                0           2
Positive → call rate        0%          33%

WHAT WE SAW
Flat week and I want to be straight with you about it rather than dress
it up.

Two things happened. First, reply volume dropped about 27%, which lines
up with the list. We're 3 weeks into the "operations director" segment
and we've now touched most of it. That segment is close to exhausted,
which is normal and expected, just arriving now.

Second, both positive replies came in Thursday afternoon and neither has
booked yet. Both are in active follow-up, one is scheduled for a call
attempt Monday. So "0 booked" is partly a timing artifact of a short
week, not purely a conversion problem.

What I'm not going to do is tell you this was a good week. It wasn't.

WHAT WE'RE DOING ABOUT IT
  → Cutting: operations director segment, it's tapped out
  → Building: new list against finance leaders at the same company
    profile. Same buyer pain, different door. 2,800 leads, going out
    for your approval today
  → Rerunning: CRM reactivation. It's been 11 weeks since the last one
    and you've been running ads in that window, so there should be
    fresh opt-ins to work. That's historically our fastest win
  → Continuing: both live prospects, phone attempts Monday

WHAT WE NEED FROM YOU
  □ Approve the finance leader list · by Monday, this is blocking launch
  □ Fresh CRM export, same 4 stages as last time · by Tuesday
```
What that report does: names the bad news first, explains it with a real mechanism instead of an excuse, separates the timing artifact from the actual problem without hiding behind it, and shows four concrete actions already underway. A client reading that knows exactly what happened and knows you are on it.

## The Loom (four minutes)

| Time | What you say |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | Headline the week in one sentence. Good or bad, say it straight |
| 0:20 | Walk the numbers on screen, week over week |
| 1:15 | Your read. Why the numbers moved the way they did |
| 2:15 | What you are scaling, cutting, and testing next |
| 3:15 | What you need from them, with a date attached |
| 3:45 | Close. What next week should look like |

A short Loom beats a written summary every single time. Same numbers, but your face and voice, and it takes four minutes instead of the twenty you would spend writing something equally good.

## Where the numbers come from

| Metric | Source |
|---|---|
| New campaigns launched | Your own count |
| Emails sent | Sequencer analytics (ListKit in the original guide) |
| Total replies | Sequencer analytics |
| Positive replies | Sequencer analytics, filtered to positive |
| Calls booked | Booked call tracking sheet |
| Positive to call rate | Calls booked ÷ positive replies |

The sequencer analytics page gives a clean breakdown you can share directly, or pull the numbers into your own branded template. Either way you are not building numbers by hand.

## Benchmarks to read against

| Metric | Concerning | Fine | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| New campaigns per month | 0 to 1 | 2 to 3 | 4+ per bundle |
| Reply rate | Under 1% | 1 to 3% | 3%+ |
| Positive reply rate | Under 0.3% | 0.3 to 1% | 1%+ |
| Positive to call booked | Under 25% | 25 to 49% | 50%+ |

Read the whole chain, not one number. Low reply rate is a targeting or deliverability problem. Good replies but low positive rate is an offer or angle problem. Good positive replies but low booking rate is not a marketing problem at all, it is inbox management, and rebuilding lists will not fix it.

## Rules
- Never skip a week. Not when results are bad, not when the client has gone quiet, not when you are busy.
- "What we're doing about it" and the analysis are the deliverable. Anyone can paste numbers. Your analysis and your actions are what they are paying for.
- Give every ask a date. "Send me the case studies" gets ignored. "Send me the case studies by Wednesday" gets done.
- Post it whether or not they reply. Silent clients are still reading, and the paper trail matters if this ever turns into a dispute.
- Never let a number be unexplained. If something moved and you do not know why, say that you do not know and say what you are doing to find out. That reads as competent. A confident wrong explanation does not survive contact with the next report.
