# Targeting Checklist

Read this when building a first list for a new client, when a segment stops producing, or when the client's ICP needs sharpening.

## Contents
1. Inputs to pull from the project

## 1. Inputs to pull from the project

Before building anything, find these. If a line is missing from the project, note it rather than filling the gap yourself.

| Input | Where it usually lives | What it decides |
|---|---|---|
| Stated ICP | Onboarding form | Base filters |
| Best 5 to 10 clients | Onboarding form, sales call | Lookalike pattern, and the proof you can cite |
| Worst clients, who they refuse | Onboarding form | Hard exclusions |
| Offer and price point | Onboarding form | Company size floor, since price sets the minimum viable buyer |
| Guarantee or risk reversal | Onboarding form | Often the strongest line for a skeptical segment |
| Definition of a qualified prospect, in their words | Onboarding form | The standard the invoice gets judged against |
| Sales process and CTA | Onboarding call | Whether the list needs to support a call ask or a lead magnet ask |
| Days from call to close | Onboarding call | How long before this segment can be judged |
| Case studies and results by segment | Assets | Which segments have proof |
| Campaign history | Slack, campaign results | What is burnt, what worked, what was never tried |
| Other channel results | Weekly check-ins | Segments already validated somewhere else, which are the cheapest wins |

## 2. The segment scoring rubric

Score each candidate 1 to 3 and lead with the highest total.

**Proof (weight this highest)**
- 3, a named case study from a near-identical company
- 2, a result from an adjacent industry with the same pain
- 1, no proof, this is a validation test

**Reachability**
- 3, one decision maker, findable by title, small company
- 2, two decision makers, titles vary by company
- 1, committee purchase or the buyer has no consistent title

**Urgency**
- 3, a real trigger, regulation, seasonality, funding, hiring signal, tech change
- 2, a chronic pain with no deadline attached
- 1, a nice-to-have

A 7 or higher is a real campaign. A 5 or 6 is worth testing at 1,000 leads. Below 5, propose something else.

## 3. Segment patterns that work

- **Trigger-based.** Recently funded, recently hired for a role that implies the pain, recently launched a product, recently changed a system.
- **Lookalike of a case study.** Same industry, same size band, same business model as a client the customer already got a result for. Then the email can name the result.
- **Second-in-command targeting.** Owners ignore cold email at higher rates than the operators who feel the pain daily. Often the better first touch, especially at 50+ employees.
- **Underserved size band.** Companies too small for the enterprise vendors and too big for the DIY tools. Real urgency, low competition.
- **Adjacent channel spillover.** A segment the client already converts through ads or referrals but has never emailed. Proof exists, targeting is proven, only the channel is new.

## 4. Segment patterns that do not

- **Everyone in an industry.** No angle survives contact with a list this broad.
- **Titles without a size filter.** A "Head of Ops" at a 12-person company and a 1,200-person company are different buyers with different budgets.
- **Segments chosen because the list was easy to build.** Convenience is not a targeting strategy.
- **Committee purchases at enterprise.** Long cycles, many stakeholders, and the booked call rarely converts, which turns into an invoice fight.
- **A segment the client already saturated on another channel.** Check before building.

## 5. Suppression checklist

Apply all of these before extraction.

- Existing customers
- Anyone currently in their pipeline or CRM as an active opportunity
- Named competitors
- Anyone the client explicitly named as off-limits
- Anyone contacted by any campaign in the last 90 days
- Anyone who replied not interested or asked to stop, on any campaign, ever
- Partner and vendor relationships the client would not want emailed cold

If the client cannot produce a suppression export, ask for it in writing once and note in the sign-off message that the list was built without one. That note is protection.

## 6. Diagnosing a dead list

A campaign with real volume and no signal. Read the failure mode before touching anything.

| Symptom | Most likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Almost no replies of any kind | Deliverability or the list is stale or unverified | Check sending health first, then list source |
| Replies, all negative, mostly "not a fit" | Wrong segment | Rebuild the list, keep the copy |
| Replies, all negative, mostly "no budget" | Company size floor too low for the price point | Raise the size band |
| "Wrong person, talk to X" repeatedly | Title targeting is off | Retarget to the title being named |
| Positive replies that never book | Not a targeting problem. Inbox management or the CTA | Leave the list alone |
| Positive replies that book and go nowhere | Qualification, not targeting | Tighten the filter the client's own definition implies |

The distinction that matters: reply-quality problems are targeting problems, and booking problems are sales problems. Do not rebuild a list to fix a follow-up issue.

## 7. The quarterly refresh

Every three months, per client:

- Re-pull the suppression list, since their pipeline has changed
- Ask what new case studies exist, because new proof unlocks new segments
- Ask what offers or markets they are testing elsewhere
- Retire any segment contacted twice with no result
- Propose one segment that has never been tried

The client changes faster than the list does. A list built on a nine-month-old ICP is a slow leak.
